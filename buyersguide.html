Step by Step
The Home Buying Process

Buying a home is a journey — and knowing each milestone makes the whole experience far less stressful. Here is exactly what to expect.

1
Pre-Approval
  • Speak with a trusted lender and gather your documents
  • Address any credit issues that may affect your rate
  • Determine the right loan type and mortgage for your situation
  • Receive your official pre-approval letter to shop with confidence
2
Home Shopping
  • Discuss your must-haves and deal-breakers with Misty
  • Tour listings in your price range and preferred areas
  • Take notes, photos, and videos at each home
  • Refine your criteria as you discover what matters most
3
Offer & Negotiation
  • Research comparable sales with your agent to find fair value
  • Submit a strong, strategically structured offer
  • Negotiate terms, timelines, and contingencies
  • Sign the purchase agreement when terms are accepted
4
Due Diligence
  • Schedule a licensed home inspector immediately after acceptance
  • Order the appraisal and survey
  • Negotiate repairs or credits based on inspection findings
  • Begin title exam and finalize your loan
5
Pre-Closing & Title
  • Confirm all agreed-upon repairs are complete
  • Conduct your final walk-through of the property
  • Wire closing funds to the title company
  • Review your closing disclosure carefully
6
Closing Day 🎉
  • Review and sign the closing statement
  • Sign all closing documents with the title company
  • Receive your keys — the home is yours!
  • You are a homeowner!

Step 1 Deep Dive
Getting Pre-Approved

Being pre-approved — unlike being pre-qualified — means a lender has actually reviewed your documented financial information and approved you for a specific loan amount. This is your first and most important step.

Know Your Budget
Know exactly how much house you can afford and stop wasting time on homes outside your range.
Negotiating Power
Sellers know you're serious. You gain a clear competitive advantage over unqualified buyers.
Close Faster
Key financial checks are already done, shortening your closing timeline.
Beat Competition
In fast-moving markets, pre-approved buyers win. Others lose deals waiting to qualify.

What NOT to Do Before Closing

✕
Make a Big Purchase
Avoid buying a car, furniture, or appliances until after closing. Large purchases change your debt-to-income ratio and can jeopardize your loan approval.
✕
Open New Credit
Do not open any new credit cards or take out any loan. New accounts lower your credit score and can trigger a full re-review of your file.
✕
Miss Any Payments
Pay every bill on time throughout the entire process. Even one missed payment can drop your score enough to affect your rate or kill your approval.
✕
Move Large Sums of Money
Avoid transferring large sums between accounts or making undocumented deposits. Both send red flags to your underwriter and delay closing.
✕
Change Jobs
Lenders verify employment right before closing. Avoid changing jobs, going self-employed, or altering your income structure during the loan process.

Step 3
Making an Offer

When we find a home you love, we will quickly and strategically place an offer. Several factors can make your offer more compelling than competing offers:

Put In a Competitive Offer
We decide on a price based on current market conditions, comparable properties recently sold nearby, the property's condition, and the level of buyer demand in that specific area.
Pay in Cash vs. Loan
Paying cash offers a faster closing timeline and fewer complications — making it far more appealing to sellers. If using financing, strong pre-approval is your next-best advantage.
Put Down a Larger Deposit
An offer with a larger earnest money deposit signals genuine commitment to the seller. It presents a more serious and competitive position than minimal deposits.
Offer a Shorter Closing Timeline
A shorter timeframe is generally more attractive to sellers who want certainty. A typical closing timeframe in Las Vegas is 30–45 days.
Write a Personal Letter
In competitive situations, a brief, sincere letter from you to the seller can create an emotional connection — and sometimes that's the deciding factor.

During the inspection period, we will schedule a thorough investigation with a reputable licensed home inspector. This is one of the most important protections available to you as a buyer.

Always attend the inspection in person. Your inspector will walk you through every finding in real time — this is your single best opportunity to understand the true condition of the home before you commit.
Step 5
Appraisal, Title & Insurance
Appraisal Ordered
Your lender arranges for a third-party appraiser to provide an independent estimate of the home's value. The appraisal confirms the price is fair and the loan amount is justified.
Property Title Search
A title search examines public records to confirm legal ownership and check for outstanding liens. It ensures clear ownership and addresses any issues before the sale — performed by a title company or real estate attorney.
What Clear Title VerifiesCommon Issues (Usually Resolved Quickly)
Legal ownership of the propertyOutstanding mortgage balances
Accuracy of the property descriptionHOA liens or unpaid assessments
Seller has the legal right to sellTax liens
No unresolved claims remain on titleErrors in public records or prior deeds
Homeowners Insurance
Required by your lender before closing. Protects against fire, storms, flooding, and liability. Get quotes from at least 2–3 providers. Even cash buyers should carry it.
Home Warranty
Covers the cost of repairing or replacing major systems and appliances from normal wear and tear. Think: A/C stops cooling, dishwasher breaks, water heater quits. Typically $400–$700/year.

Avoid These Pitfalls
Common New Homeowner Mistakes
1
Ignoring Home Maintenance
Budget 1–2% of your home's value annually for upkeep: cleaning gutters, servicing HVAC, power washing, and general repairs. Small issues become expensive if ignored.
2
Making Major Renovations Too Soon
Live in the space before committing to large-scale renovations. What you think you want on day one often changes after a few months of actually living there.
3
Not Budgeting for Additional Expenses
Beyond the mortgage: property taxes, HOA fees, utilities, insurance, landscaping, and repair materials add up fast. Plan for it so there are no surprises.
4
Not Changing the Locks
You never know how many key copies exist. Re-key or replace every exterior lock immediately after closing — it's inexpensive and provides instant peace of mind.
5
Skipping the Homestead Declaration
Most Nevada homeowners never file this. It protects up to $605,000 in equity from unsecured creditors and takes less than 30 minutes. Ask Misty for the form.
